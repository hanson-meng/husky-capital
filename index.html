§ I · Mandate
I
Mandate

A finance club that produces something, not one that meets.

Most school finance clubs read the news and argue about stocks. That is not what this is.

Husky Capital runs on the assumption that the technical work is learnable at sixteen if somebody lays it out in order. So we laid it out in order: valuation, the three statements, comparables, discounted cash flow, precedent transactions, leveraged buyouts, merger models. Members work through it together, weekly, and are expected to be able to build the thing rather than describe it.

§ III · Coverage
III
Coverage

One company a term, written up properly.

The note is the club’s output and the reason the syllabus exists. Members pick a listed company, build the model, and write the argument. It is published under Husky Capital’s name with the authors on the front, which means nobody can hide behind the group.

First note in preparation

Every note carries a section on what would make the thesis wrong. A club note that only argues one side is a press release, and the members writing it learn nothing from it.

The model behind each note is kept and handed on, so the second year of the club starts from the first year’s work rather than from scratch.

Notes are student work, produced for the purpose of learning. Nothing published here is investment advice or a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

§ IV · Join
IV · Join

If you want to learn how a company is actually valued,come.

No prior finance background is expected and none is required. The syllabus starts at the beginning on purpose. What the club does expect is that you turn up weekly and that you do the reading, because the sessions build on each other and a member who skips module 5 cannot follow module 10.

Bring a laptop. Most of what we do happens in a spreadsheet.
